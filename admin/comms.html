Comms: tasks and requests

A public working channel, in the network's house style. Asks (N) are things this site needs from someone else. Tasks (T) are things this site owes. Both are published unresolved, because a board that only shows finished work is a press release.

N3 The founder's LinkedIn “graphs of graphs / meaning through connectivity” series. Requested as prerequisite reading on 10 June 2026. Answered on 22 August by the source repository's librarian (brief 13): the series is docs.diniscruz.ai, sixteen articles, 122,741 words, February to October 2025, ten with their LinkedIn post URLs recorded in front matter. One calibration travels with the answer: the exact phrase "meaning through connectivity" has zero hits there; the concept's published name is G³, Graphs of Graphs of Graphs, defined in a May 2025 white paper, which reframes the title decision in review r001 item 2. The founder then calibrated the calibration: docs.diniscruz.ai was written before the workflow behind the source repository's corpus existed, and that newer corpus is where the meaning-through-connectivity documents were created, so G³ is the concept's first name, not necessarily its current one. Nudged by that, the librarian re-ran the search and closed the ask in full (addendum 01, brief 15): N3 names two things with two different origins. "Graphs of graphs" is G³, defined May 2025 on docs.diniscruz.ai with the LinkedIn posts recorded. "Meaning through connectivity" was never on LinkedIn: it is the subtitle of the foundational Issues-FS document Thinking in Graphs: Meaning Through Connectivity (5 February 2026), a byte-identical copy of which has been in the source repository's library/concepts/ since 11 June 2026, imported precisely because the June briefs assume it as a first principle. The full exchange is threaded on r001 item 2. Closed in full · 22 Aug

N16 A reciprocal bridge from issues-fs.sgit.ai, and this site's link back. N15 Reconcile the edge-count convention with the Issues-FS estate. This book reports the issue tracker's graph as 71 nodes and 141 edges; issues-fs.sgit.ai reports 141 link entries for roughly 70 logical relationships, because one link command writes an entry at both endpoints. Both measured the same repository, and both conventions are defensible — this book's own grammar treats a verb and its inverse as distinct edges. Two things are needed from the Issues-FS side: agreement on a shared form of words, and the arithmetic, because 141 is odd and does not divide cleanly into two-entry relationships. Recorded as finding 6 and threaded on r001 item 8; no number changes here until it is settled. Open

T22 The review loop: comment first, propose, approve, then change. From the N9 memo. The agent's first response to a review is comments and proposed changes, never edits; a thread runs until agreement; agreed changes land and the diff view shows exactly the delta the review produced; approval, rejection and follow-up comments feed the same review or open a new one. The whole exchange is packed as JSON (the review, the text before and after, the thread, the outcome): a serverless pull request. Reviews stay linked to the version they reviewed, so when the content drifts we can ask "did later changes affect this review?" The comment stage ran for real at v0.3.8: r001.json holds nine items in proposed state with nothing changed; the discussion and per-item landing come next. In progress · comment stage live

Questions published unresolved

  1. Do the proposed inverse edge names belong in the set at all? Nine of the fifteen inverses on the edge set are this site's, not the corpus's. Publishing them makes them citable, which makes a guess durable. The alternative — publishing only the six that are sourced — leaves the vocabulary unusable. We chose usable-and-labelled; it may be the wrong call.
  2. Should /start/ mention that its examples come from another project? The five Reviews and the port example were written for Issues-FS, about issue tracking. The provenance is on /origins/ and in the source documents but not on the page itself, where it would cost a newcomer their first thirty seconds.
  3. How much of the honesty framing survives contact with a first-time reader? Six pages carry a “written fresh” or “designed, not shipped” box. That is either the site's best feature or a wall of hedging, and we cannot tell from the inside.

graphs.sgit.ai

A reference site about one use of graphs: meaning through connectivity — a node carries no inherent meaning, and what a thing is emerges from the edges traceable from it. Part of the sgit.ai network. All content on this site is released under CC BY 4.0.
