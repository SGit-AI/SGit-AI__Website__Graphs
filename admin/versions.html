Release history

v0.3.24 23 August 2026 The sources this book was built from, carried whole. A new section, /docs/, holding fifteen documents and 39,973 words of the founder's own architecture briefs — the material the book was distilled from, most of it never published anywhere a reader could reach. They are carried, not summarised: each .md is a byte-for-byte copy from the-cyber-boardroom/SGraph-AI__App__Send (public, Apache-2.0) at commit b561fa6b14, with its SHA-256 printed on the page and the build failing if a carried copy stops matching its hash, because the copy is evidence and evidence is not edited. Each document gets an intro, a summary of what it says, a note on what is worth re-reading, an in-page reader over the carried markdown, and a graph of where it lands on this site. Three kinds of link, kept apart and drawn apart. The documents are carried; the concept links are measured (every one of the concept map's twenty-four concepts has a declared phrase list, published on the page, counted mechanically, dashed in the graph and labelled with its count); the site links are authored (which chapter, ladder layer, vault or page rests on it, each with a note saying what the link is for, drawn solid). Nothing pretends the third kind is the second. Influence follows by a stated formula — concepts + 2 × places + 3 × review asks resolved — and the page states what it is not: it measures load on this site, not quality, and a document absorbed so completely that nothing cites it any more would score near zero. The consolidated view draws all fifteen joined by the concepts they share, which is the only view that shows the corpus's own shape rather than one drawn by hand: the one concept measured in all fifteen is a claim is worth its chain of custody. And a correction the work forced. Building the readers surfaced that r004 decision D5 rests on a false premise: it says Cytoscape was this site's first third-party dependency, but every /documents/ page has loaded marked from a content delivery network since that section shipped, and mdreader.js still imports Mermaid from one. A network dependency is worse for the property at stake than a vendored file, so the count was not one, it was three, and two of them were the wrong kind. Marked is now vendored (MIT, 35 KB) and no page fetches it at read time; the Mermaid import stays, is best-effort, and is stated in LICENSES.md alongside both vendored libraries rather than being silent. The decision's question is left standing with the correction printed beside it, which is the corpus's own supersede, never delete rule applied to this site's own record. No book content changed.
v0.3.23 23 August 2026 The date on the agent surface was three releases stale, and nothing was watching it. llms.txt opens with Site version: vX.Y.Z (date), and every agent that reads this site reads that line first. chrome.py has stamped the version there since v0.2.4 — precisely because hand-editing it had silently missed twice on a sibling site — but it never stamped the date, so the date sat at 21 August through v0.3.20, v0.3.21 and v0.3.22 while the versions beside it moved. The fix is the same shape as the original: the date is now read from the release's own row in the history table rather than stamped as today, so rebuilding an old release does not re-date it, and a new gate check fails the build when the two disagree. This is the second time on this site that a value was correct in one place, copied by hand into another, and drifted — and the second time the answer was to make one place own it and let the gate enforce the agreement. No content changed.
v0.3.22 23 August 2026 Three risk vaults, the first cross-vault finding, and the decisions register as a graph. Two founder requests in one release. First, the evidence estate goes wider. Risk Mandate (124 files, 98 commits, eight entry points into one mandate), Agentic Browser Isolation (104 files, 17 entry points, and the same risk written seven times, once per stakeholder, none of them a summary of another) and the Risk Graph Explorer (33 files, permissions: {}, where an empty register is a correct answer) join the two vaults published yesterday, on the same terms: the vaults' own published screenshots re-published under CC BY 4.0 with source and fetch date, their derived facts quoted rather than recounted, and a what the book will cite block on every page. And the programme produced its first finding that no single case study contains: the capability scale, read out of five permission blocks side by side, four of them from vaults analysed here and the fifth marked as quoted — a floor (permissions: {}, nothing requested at all), a genuinely hard middle (read one folder, write one folder) and a ceiling (a model call whose key is sealed into the vault rather than held by the reader). That is the argument for going deep rather than writing five summaries, and it is now evidence rather than a claim. Second, the decisions register gets a page of its own. /decisions/ draws each of the fourteen decisions — ten of them open — as the peak of its own graph: the options hang below it, the side effect of choosing each option hangs below that, and the work it blocks and the pages it touches run out to the sides. Switch to the whole-register view and a second thing appears that no list could show: four of the twelve named pieces of work have more than one decision waiting on them, and every one of the four is named differently by each decision — the same task is level 4 completion in one place and level 4 rollout under D1 in another, because the two decisions were raised in different reviews and each described the block in its own review's vocabulary. That count is computed on every build, not asserted: each block phrase carries a shared key, and a phrase the generator has never seen fails the build, which turns “is this new work?” into a question the build asks for you. The page is generated by gen_decisions.py straight from each review file's decisions array, so it cannot drift from the reviews, and the build fails if an open decision has no options authored under it. The mechanism is borrowed intact from the browser-isolation vault analysed in the same release: one named owner, no deny button (answer, defer with a reason, or say what data would make it answerable), nothing moves until it is taken personally, and the reason travels with the answer. Answers are held in the reader's own browser and copied back as plain text — a site with no server should not grow a form endpoint to collect decisions. No book content changed.
v0.3.21 22 August 2026 The path query, and contradictions as measurements. Two answers to one memo. First: the previous release declined to build a path-query UI because it would make the book's honesty table wrong; the founder asked why that stopped it, and the honest answer was that nothing technical did — the caution was about a content claim, not a capability. So it is built: a pattern is a start filter plus up to three edge-and-node steps, matches print as sentences using each edge's verb or its inverse depending on which way the route walks it, cycles cannot spin, and the result is capped at 300 and says when it hit the cap, because a query that silently truncates is worse than one that refuses. Five worked patterns ship as starting points, including unevidenced concepts in units a finding touches. The page then states its own falsification in full: chapter 12 lists the path-query language under what does not exist, something now executes path patterns, and the precise damage is spelled out — the corpus's seventeen queries over the semantic graph still do not run, while a generic matcher over this book's own ladder does. Exact replacement wording is proposed on the page and recorded as r004 decision D6; chapter 12 changes only when the founder agrees it, so no reader is misled in the meantime because the correction sits beside the thing that caused it. Second: the reviewer's framing that conflicts becoming deterministic and detectable is the powerful part, and that having them is not automatically wrong, reshaped the findings register. Findings now carry a state — three open, five accepted — and an accepted one records why, so the acceptance can be argued with rather than assumed; one is accepted with a condition and returns to open if v0.4.0 ships without growing the fractal treatment. And five checks now run on every build, each stating the rule it ran: strong concepts with no published demonstration, units two independent findings both touch, concepts nothing rests on, level 3 and 4 text the concept layer has not reached, and oppositions landing in one passage. A check with zero hits is kept, because a rule with no hits is still a rule. No book content changed.
v0.3.20 22 August 2026 The concept layer, and a graph you can explore from anywhere. Reviewer feedback that the ontology work captured structure but not concepts: "it's kind of like a dictionary and a thesaurus at a three-dimensional level". So the ladder gains a layer it did not have. The concept map holds 24 concepts, each with a definition, what it is also called, what it is near but importantly not, and typed edges to other concepts — grounds, specialises, constrains, enables, opposes, every one with a distinct inverse written into the data rather than inferred, because a concept map is exactly where the banned generic edge tempts you. A concept crosses the ladder sideways: it names the units at several altitudes that carry it at once, consolidated above and expanded below, which is the intersection the ladder alone could not show. Peaks are computed, not chosen: strength is a stated formula (out + 2×incoming + units + 2×demonstrations) so the ranking can be disagreed with by recomputing it. The top three came out as meaning through connectivity, confidence is a function of connectivity, and a claim is worth its chain of custody. And a first inventory of what the book reduces to: 14 facts, 26 assertions, 0 opinions, derived from evidence state rather than hand-typed, with the honest note that a proper register is r002 item 4's job. The graph explorer was rebuilt around the same feedback: labels inside the node with a wrap control, force layout with an iterations slider and a run-until-settled pass, radius exploration from any node (double-click to centre), collapse a selection into a group and expand it again, paths read as sentences using each edge's verb or its inverse depending on which way the path walks it, full screen, hide-the-panels, drag-resizable panes, PNG export, and a view file carrying settings, zoom, pan, collapsed groups and every hand-moved node position, so a picture can be recreated rather than only kept. And the concept layer immediately produced finding 8: schema-first thinking — the position the book exists to argue against — comes out among the computed peaks, because five separate concepts define themselves partly by opposing it. Kept rather than tuned away, with the caution it carries: a strong node is not a supported one, and any future ranking that conflates connectivity with evidence will overstate whatever the book repeats most often.

v0.3.18 22 August 2026 The flicker, fixed; and the ladder drawn as one graph. Two pieces of reviewer feedback. The bug: every click rebuilt the entire column strip and then smooth-scrolled it to the far end, so re-opening a level that was already on screen flickered the whole view. Now only the columns to the right of the click are rebuilt, the scroll position is preserved, and the strip scrolls only far enough to bring a genuinely off-screen column into view. Where nothing needs to move, the changed column gives a brief pulse instead — feedback without motion, which was the other half of the complaint. The graph: a new page, the ladder as one graph, drawn with Cytoscape.js vendored into the site rather than loaded from a CDN. Configurable by levels, node kinds (units, findings, claims), colouring (altitude, taxonomy class, evidence) and layout. It shows what columns cannot: 42 cross-references between chapters, measured from the book's own markdown rather than asserted, running sideways across a compression tree of 35 edges — the book is far less of a tree than its contents page suggests. One design note worth reading on the page: a contradiction is drawn as a node, not an edge, because an edge meaning "contradicts" would have to be its own inverse and this book's grammar bans self-inverse edges. The findings are reified and flags the units they involve, which is the grammar chapter applied to a picture of the book itself. No book content changed.
v0.3.17 22 August 2026 The ladder becomes columns, and every level gets an ontology. Reviewer feedback on the r004 experiment, acted on in three parts. Navigation: opening a unit no longer replaces the view — it appends a resizable column beside it, so the chain from the one-paragraph book down to a section stays on screen as the tree it is, and the columns container scrolls rather than the page. Every column carries an index of all units at its level, grouped by that level's taxonomy, so level 3 can be entered directly instead of climbed down to, with its ancestors rebuilt in the columns to its left. The URL carries the whole trail, so a path is linkable, not just a node. Colour: the descending links now mean something switchable — the evidence behind the unit below, how far it descends, or its taxonomy class — because a descent link usefully carries at least three signals and hard-coding one would have been schema-first. Ontology and taxonomy per level: each level now carries both, kept apart as the glossary keeps them (taxonomy points upward, ontology points outward), with every edge type a verb with a distinct inverse and a stated domain and range. That produced finding 7, the most interesting result since the database contradiction: the classes do not survive the climb. A section classed Rule at level 4 sits inside a chapter classed Prescription at level 3, inside a part classed Argument at level 2, and nothing inherits; the vocabulary changes kind, not granularity; and at level 1 the taxonomy collapses, because a taxonomy of one unit is not a taxonomy. It argues against a single book-wide ontology and warns r002 item 3 that its node types will be level-dependent. Review r004 gains items 7 and 8 (both applied) and decision D4: confirm the authored classes, or correct them. No book content changed.

v0.3.15 22 August 2026 The altitude ladder: one book at five altitudes. How a version is decided

  • Every push to dev is a minor bump — vR.M.N → vR.M.(N+1). A deliberate major goes to vR.(M+1).0; CI accepts either and rejects anything else.
  • The version lives in one file. admin/build/version.txt. chrome.py propagates it to every page badge, to llms.txt, to llms-full.txt and to index.md; validate.js fails the build if any of them disagree.
  • It must be in the commit subject — site vX.Y.Z: …. CI anchors the tag to that commit, which is HEAD on a direct push and HEAD's parent when a pull request lands as a merge.
  • A row here is required. The build fails if this table has no row for the current version, or lists any version twice.
  • A correction that changes a claim gets a row, not a silent edit.
