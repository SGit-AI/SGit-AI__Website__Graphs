Fractal semantic graphs

A node means nothing on its own.
What it is arrives through its edges.

If you followed the phrase here, this is the whole of it: the same structural pattern — nodes with edges, meaning through connectivity — repeats at every level of zoom. Semantic, because meaning comes from the edges rather than from the label on the box. Fractal, because zooming into any node lands you in another semantic graph with its own node types, verbs and taxonomy, joined by a named edge to the one you left. The grammar survives every zoom. The vocabulary is meant to change.

Quoted, not paraphrased: Thinking in Graphs: Meaning Through Connectivity, section “Every Scope Defines Its Own Vocabulary”, carried whole on this site and checked byte for byte on every build. The same idea was called graphs of graphs of graphs, and ontologies of ontologies of ontologies, before it had this name. This page is the door; everything on the site is one page along.

This is the corpus’s own worked example, live. Two nodes hold the value 8080: one is connected to a type, which is constrained, which is inherited, which belongs to a released library. The other is connected to nothing. Add the edges one at a time and watch the list on the right grow. Drag a node if you want to be sure this is a graph and not a picture of one.

“The difference is not in the value.” Both scenarios hold 8080. “The difference is in the connectivity.” Every claim above is backed by a traceable path through the graph, which is the only reason the graph may make it. Read the section this comes from.

Fractal is a test, not a flourish

The test has two halves and both are worth running. If the inside has the same types and verbs as the outside, all the way down, you have a hierarchy, and a folder tree is the clean example. If the inside needs a different grammar, so that it is no longer a semantic graph, the claim is false. In between, every zoom that opens a new vocabulary joined by a named edge is the claim working.

This estate’s own making-of book at v0.2.0, taken apart six levels and put back byte-for-byte or the build fails: 160 shards, one format, one validator. That is scale invariance, and it is the honest label, because the vocabulary never changes on the way down. By the first half of the test, this ladder on its own is a hierarchy. The book says so about itself, in the chapter that states the test. Open it and zoom it yourself →

So where is the fractal part? In the vocabulary changing. The same pilot document on this site carries two graphs at once: a core graph of sections, blocks, sentences and words, and an extraction of concepts, claims, hypotheses and objections joined by verbs like departs-from and licenses. Neither is derived from the other; they share nodes by anchor. Open a word in one and you are in a lexical world, open a claim in the other and you are in an argumentative one. The chapter runs the test on this estate and reports where it fails.
The meaning of a node is supplied by the ontology at the altitude where it sits, which is why the same node means different things at different altitudes. Article 9 is a binding provision in a graph of instruments, a container of paragraphs inside the regulation, and a source of definitions to the paragraph that cites it. Same node, different edges around it at each level.
Nobody is forced to conform. An organisation, a division, a team, a person or a regulator can each define their own world in their own words and connect by drawing edges rather than by adopting a shared schema. That is the consequence of don’t merge vocabularies, bridge them: shared facts owned by nobody, formulas per party, declared bridges between them.

The fullest worked demonstration is not on this site. sgit.ai walks eleven altitudes across seven published vaults, from the text of a law to a threat on a compute instance, each rung modelled by its own author in its own vocabulary and every one openable with the read key on its page. Markdown twin for agents: index.md. Four of those rungs are now read and gated here, in the evidence estate, alongside the ladder itself.

What this is not

The corpus’s own caveats, which travel with its ideas.

It is not a graph database pitch. The claim is that one grammar is the interface at every boundary, not that things are stored in a graph. There is no graph database, no SPARQL and no RDF behind this site. The chapter that separates what ships from what is argued is What ships, what is argued.
Nine of the edge inverses are proposals, not quotations. They are this site’s own suggestions rather than anything the corpus says, and they are marked as such everywhere they appear.
The books are under review, and say so. A book below v1.0.0 is openly unfinished; v1.0.0 is reserved for a book’s actual final release, and a gate enforces that in both directions.

graphs.sgit.ai
