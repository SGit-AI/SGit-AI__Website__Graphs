  <h1>The sgit.ai network</h1>
  <p class="lead">Nineteen focused sites on <code>*.sgit.ai</code>, each taking one question further than a section here could. They share this site's design and its discipline — sourced claims, a stated status, honest edges — and they publish their arguments <b>before</b> the things they describe exist, so the commitments stay checkable afterwards.</p>
  <p class="small dim">18 live, 1 with the repository and subdomain in place but nothing published yet. Screenshots are of the real sites, captured on the date each entry gives.</p>

        <span class="net-tag">The non-functional requirements, from the inside.</span>
        <span class="net-sum">The requirements nobody writes down until they are violated — resilience, budgets, project management, backups — treated as a reality system rather than a checklist. Includes an honest column: a scorecard, and a page naming backups as a gap.</span>
      </a>

    <div class="netcard">
      <a class="net-main" href="https://risks.sgit.ai" rel="noopener" target="_blank">
        <b>risks.sgit.ai</b>
        <span class="net-tag">You cannot deny a risk. You can only say how long you accept it.</span>
        <span class="net-sum">Risk management inverted: a named human underwrites an exposure that already exists, insurance-style, with personal liability attached. There is no deny button — the only choice is how long, and the interval is the decision. 42 concepts, a six-rung interval ladder, and a stated zero lines of code implementing any of it.</span>

        <b>standards.sgit.ai</b>
        <span class="net-tag">Point at the provision, or you are asserting.</span>
        <span class="net-sum">Laws, standards and frameworks as addressable graphs rather than PDFs to paraphrase. They are built from the same generator and hold to the same rules, so a reader moving between them is not changing register.</p>
