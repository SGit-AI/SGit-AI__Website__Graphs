Fractal Semantic Graphs: Meaning Through Connectivity

The Universe. An atlas of the ideas this estate is built from, each one with its anchor. Not the argument: the universe the argument stands on.

Concepts90, in 8 regions of one graph Connections168 named verbs · 17 anchored in a quote, 151 authored with their reasoning Quotes165 verbatim, from 35 sources, every one re-read out of its source on every build Findings6 contradictions · 9 named absences · 23 near-but-nots Printthe whole volume as a PDF · self-contained, reads offline start to finish Machineuniverse.json · the atlas as data, in the pilot extraction's shape Built2026-08-26 against estate v0.5.14 · CC BY 4.0

What this is, in one paragraph. The estate proved a method on one document: pull out what it says, anchor every extracted claim to a verbatim quote at known bytes, connect the claims with named verbs, and the document becomes something you can ask questions of. This volume runs that method over the whole corpus, and prints the result as a book. Every entry carries its quote and its source. Every connection says whether the corpus stated it or this volume's author drew it. And the two chapters that matter most are the ones about where the corpus contradicts itself and where it says nothing at all.

How to check any of it

Everything is in this folder and nothing in it needs privileged access.

  • data/universe.json — every concept, edge, alias, distinction, contradiction and gap, with the source of each anchor and the SHA-256 of every carried source at the time this book was built.
  • build/validate.py — the gate. It re-reads all 165 quotes out of the sources they name, checks both ends of every edge, refuses any edge stored under an inverse verb, and fails on an orphan.
  • build/gen_chapters.py --check — fails if a chapter has drifted from the data.
  • the markdown — the source of truth for the text. These pages render it in your browser and the PDF is printed from it, so neither can disagree with it.
  • figures/figures.json — the 8 figures, each naming the page it photographs and the estate version it was taken at.

Written by an AI, and disclosed. This volume was written by Claude, an AI system made by Anthropic, as one session of a three-book commission. The session read the corpus, chose the concepts, wrote the prose, authored the graph and built the gate that checks it. The selection is a judgement and it is the author's; the anchors are not a judgement and are checked mechanically; the authored edges are the softest material here and are marked as such.
