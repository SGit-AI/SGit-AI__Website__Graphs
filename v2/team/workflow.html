The state map

The states a change moves through here. Each names the one role that can advance it and the door: the condition the next role will not take the work without. Nine states, 7 of them a stage of the seven-stage change control, and 1 door currently shut.

Which doors are shut is counted, not written down. A door is shut when work is waiting at it and nothing has ever gone through, and both halves are read off the board on every build. The meanings and the doors are authored in workflow.json, because what a state means is a judgement; what is sitting in it is not.
A gate compares the drawing to the declaration. This page draws an order, so the seven stage-bearing states must match, in sequence, the stages admin/build/gen_board.py declares. Reorder one and forget the other and the build fails, rather than the map going on confidently drawing last month's process. Borrowed, with the reasoning, from newsroom.sgit.ai's brief 10.

The shut door

Approved has 2 item(s) waiting at it and nothing has ever passed through it. It is advanced by the founder, and its door is: The founder answers. There is no substitute, no timeout and no default, which is the point of separating proposing from choosing.

Waiting: WS-01, WS-02. Everything downstream of it — Implemented, Accepted — has therefore never been entered by any pack, which does not mean nothing has shipped: it means what shipped went around the workflow rather than through it.

How to read a shut door

A door with nothing through it is not a criticism of whoever owns it. It is the honest shape of the process at this moment, and it is worth drawing for the reason the sibling site gives: before their state map existed, one shut door was four separate caveats on four separate pages. Ours was the same — the same fact was stated on the board, in two pack records, in the issue tree and in four replies.

What a shut door does NOT mean is that nothing downstream has happened. It means nothing has passed through the workflow. Work can and does go around a shut door, and when it does, the map shows the door still shut while the release table shows the change shipped. That gap is the useful part: it says the process and the practice have diverged, and which of the two to fix is a decision rather than a bug.

For an agent

workflow.json is the machine surface: id, label, lane, seq, stage, owner, means, door and exits. The occupancy fields on this page (at, passed, shut) are computed at build time from the board and are not in the file — read them from the board rather than caching them here. To add a state, add it to workflow.json with its door and its exits; the build refuses a state with no door, an exit that names nothing, or a stage sequence that disagrees with the declared workflow.
