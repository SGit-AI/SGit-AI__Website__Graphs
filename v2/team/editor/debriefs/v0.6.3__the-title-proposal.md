# Debrief: the title proposal

**Brief** the naming question, brief 40, through the change-control plan
**Input** `../../researcher/debriefs/v0.6.3__map-the-making-of-book.md`
**Output** four candidates, each with the audience it serves and what it costs. The
recommendation is the editor's; the decision is the founder's.

---

## The constraint the map imposes

A title built from words the book does not use will be the same mistake again, in a new
direction. So every candidate below is built from the book's **top ten** vocabulary:
release (227), graph (193), book (182), agent (173), brief (120), test (87), memo (76),
ship (61), version (54), gate (47), loop (32).

Two words the founder reached for in brief 40 fail that test and are deliberately not used:
*workflow* (8 uses) and *fractal* (9, six of them naming the other book). *Scaffolding* and
*feedback loop* appear **zero** times. They are the right ideas and the wrong evidence: if
the next edition is to be about them, they have to be **written in**, not printed on the
cover.

## The subtitle carries the audience, not the title

The map found that the body is villager-facing (one code block in twelve chapters) and the
appendix is explorer-facing (21% of the words). The book already serves both, unevenly.
A title cannot hold both audiences; **a subtitle can**, and that is the cheapest way to
resolve the tension the founder named.

## The candidates

### A. *Writing a Book with an Agent*
**Subtitle:** *The loop, the gates, and eighty-eight releases in six days*
Plain, uses the book's three biggest words, says exactly what happened. Serves the villager
first, the explorer through the subtitle's specificity. **Cost:** unglamorous; sounds like
a how-to, which the body largely is and the appendix is not.

### B. *The Loop*
**Subtitle:** *How one book was written with an agent, in six days and eighty-eight releases*
The book's own central image, and its fourth-most-used structural word. Short enough to be
a spine. **Cost:** *The Loop* alone means nothing on a store page; it leans entirely on the
subtitle, and Leanpub truncates.

### C. *Gates Buy Speed*
**Subtitle:** *Building the tools while writing the book*
Takes the book's own best chapter title and its most defensible claim, and pairs it with
the founder's *"building the ship as we're preparing to fly"*. Serves the explorer: it
promises an argument, not a manual. **Cost:** it promises an argument the body only half
makes — the map found *scaffolding* and *feedback loop* absent. Choosing this means
committing to a content pass, not just a rename.

### D. *Ship, Then Improve the Ship*
**Subtitle:** *Writing a book and building its tools at the same time*
The founder's own metaphor made active, using *ship* (61). **Cost:** the metaphor is
memorable in speech and ambiguous in print — *ship* reads as a noun first on a cover, and
this is the one candidate whose meaning depends on knowing the phrase it came from.

## Recommendation

**A, unless the founder wants a content pass, in which case C.**

The reasoning is the same in both directions. A title is a promise, and the map says the
book currently keeps a *how it was done* promise better than a *why it works* one. **A**
promises what the book delivers today, and can ship with no content change and no version
move. **C** promises what brief 40 says the book is really about — and would be honest only
after the writer has put the scaffolding argument into the body, which is a real revision
and moves the book's version.

**B** is the strongest title and the weakest store listing; it would need the founder to
accept a cover that says almost nothing without its subtitle. **D** should be a chapter
title, not a book title.

## What this is not

This is not a recommendation to keep the current title. Every candidate beats it, because
the current one names something the book uses once and not about itself.

## Open, for the founder

1. **A or C** — which is the same question as: is the next edition a rename or a revision?
2. If C: the writer needs a brief to put *scaffolding is more important than the code* into
   the body, with the estate's own evidence. That is the content change brief 40 gestures
   at with *"a couple changes to the book structure"*.
3. Neither candidate carries *Claude*. Brief 40 says *"this book then is more about writing
   a book with Claude"*. The map notes the book names it four times. If the founder wants
   the tool named on the cover, that is a positioning decision the evidence does not make
   for us, and **A** takes it most easily: *Writing a Book with Claude*.
