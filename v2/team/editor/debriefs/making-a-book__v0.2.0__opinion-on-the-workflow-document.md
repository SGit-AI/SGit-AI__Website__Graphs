# Opinion: the workflow document

**Asked** whether the document says one thing, and whether it earns its place
**Subject** `v2/universe/docs/agentic-workflows/source.md`
**Role** editor, opinion only on this change

---

**Supports adding it. Supports connecting it to the book. Two edits requested, neither
blocking.**

## Does it say one thing

Yes, and it can be said in a sentence: *a workflow is how you operate, it is now cheap to
change, and you know it is mature when you stop wanting to.* Everything in the eight parts
serves that, including Part 5 on source materials, which arrives from another memo and
earns its place because the determinism boundary is what makes a workflow changeable
without being disposable.

The strongest part is Part 4. **The maturity test is the best idea in the corpus this
month**, and it is one the estate has been failing to state: a stretch of releases that
each changed the machinery is the cost, not the achievement.

## The two edits

1. **Part 8 is the weakest.** *The Loop, End to End* restates what the book's chapter 1
   already does better, with fewer scenes. It survives because *What It Does Not Buy* is
   worth keeping. If the document is ever cut for length, cut *One Round* and keep the
   other two sections.

2. **The title carries a colon it may not need.** *Agentic Workflows: How You Operate*
   matches the companion document's shape, which is the argument for it. Against it: *How
   You Operate* is the definition, so the title states the thing and its definition at
   once, which is slightly redundant. The editor would keep it, and notes the objection so
   the founder can overrule.

## On the book title, which this changes

The retitle at v0.6.9 shipped with an honest caveat on the record: the title *"is not yet
true of the book as written"*, because the book used the word six times.

**This document does not make it true.** A reference document sitting in the universe is
not the book. The title becomes true when the writer's step 4 lands, and the editor's
opinion is that the two should be treated as one piece of work with two versions rather
than as a rename that happened to be followed by a revision. The record should say so
plainly, because otherwise the estate has a book named after a concept that lives
somewhere else.

## What the editor would refuse

Writing the vocabulary into the book **before** part one exists. Brief 42 and brief 45 both
put the *Art of the Possible* first. Establishing the vocabulary in a new early chapter and
then writing part one in front of it would mean the reader meets an abstract frame before
they have seen anything worth framing. That is the mistake the founder has twice asked the
estate not to make.
