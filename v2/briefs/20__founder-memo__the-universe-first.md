# Founder memo: build the universe first, then find the plot

**Date:** 23 August 2026 · **Source:** voice memo, transcribed live by otter.ai and reproduced
verbatim below (per the house rule: the founder's voice is source material and is not edited)
· **Recorded while reading** [dev pack v0.3.27](../dev-pack/index.html), at section 5
· **Status:** it reshapes the pack. What it changes is listed after the transcript.

> **Where the earlier briefs are.** Briefs 00 to 19 belong to the first edition and froze with
> it at [v0.3.26](../admin/versions.html); they are at `/v1/briefs/`. This directory holds the
> briefs written after the freeze. The numbering continues rather than restarting, because the
> corpus is one sequence even though the editions are not.

**What it says, in one paragraph:** the pack is defining answers before the questions are known,
and section 2 is the clearest case: it hands every unit of the first edition a carry / lift /
rewrite / drop verdict before anyone knows what shape the second book is. The best thing in the
pack is the six node families, and the memo's central claim is that **nothing about the levels
can be known until that universe exists**. So the construction order inverts: not spine first
and evidence attached to it, but **all the reference material first**, decomposed into concepts,
facts, claims and evidence, and only then the plot lines drawn through it. A book is a journey
with pace, punchlines and characters, and the question the author is really answering is a
whodunit: what is the sequence, what is the story, what does the reader need in chapter one that
chapter two will spend. Three specific corrections follow: **fractal semantic graphs does not
mean one grammar everywhere**, it means semantic graphs nested in a fractal way, graphs of
graphs, ontologies of ontologies, with local override where a scope needs it, exactly as the
Issues-FS lexicon works; **Wardley maps are not an appendix**, because a map is a graph that has
gained position, movement and time, which is what makes a graph actionable, and a map of a map
of a map is the fractal claim in its most legible form; and **the five spine statements are not
yet the answer**, they are a guess to be arrived at rather than assumed. The frame for all of it
is explorer to villager and back to explorer: the first pass ran to something near a product,
it has been frozen, and this pass starts again with better tools rather than from scratch.

## The correction to *fractal semantic graphs*

The pack defined it as **uniformity**: one grammar, one validator, one query engine at every
altitude. The memo says that is not the claim.

The claim is **composition with local override**:

> Semantic graphs nested in a fractal way. Graphs of graphs of graphs. Ontologies of ontologies.
> A lexicon exists and is well connected, and any scope may extend, specialise or override it
> where it needs to, dynamically, without asking permission of the centre.

These are genuinely different claims. Uniformity is a constraint that makes things comparable.
Composition with override is a **capability** that makes local meaning survive without
fragmenting the whole. The second is the interesting one, and it is the one the corpus already
argues: *Issues-FS Lexicon*, carried at `/v1/docs/issues-fs-lexicon.html`, says in its own words
that the Lexicon is **not** a schema registry and **not** the authoritative source of
definitions, and that any scope can extend, specialise or override a bootstrap definition. The
carried source contradicted the pack's definition and nobody noticed, which is a finding about
the pack rather than about the source.

Uniformity has not disappeared. It is a consequence: what makes the override safe is that the
grammar for expressing an override is the same at every level. But it is the mechanism, not the
claim, and the pack had them the wrong way round.

## The correction on Wardley maps

The audit dropped chapter 11 from the spine on the grounds that nothing in the concept map
depends on it. The memo gives the reason that was missing, and it is a strong one:

- A **map is a graph that has gained position, movement and time.** Position is meaningful,
  movement is the point, and time is expressible in a way it is not in a plain graph.
- A **map of a map of a map** is the fractal claim in its most legible form, which makes maps a
  demonstration of the title rather than a digression from it.
- The evolution axis is **not** fixed to genesis-to-commodity. Other evolutions exist and can be
  mapped, which generalises the technique.
- Maps are what make a graph **actionable**: strategy and gameplay are things you do to a map.

The honest summary of the relationship, in the founder's own framing: *you cannot have maps
without graphs, but maps are where graphs take on meaning, positioning and gameplay that a graph
alone does not have.* That is a claim about the whole book's subject, and it belongs in the
universe as a candidate spine statement rather than in an appendix queue.

## Two proposals this memo makes possible

Neither is in the memo. Both follow from it and both are computable, which is the test this
project applies to its own ideas.

### The pacing gate

Instruction 7 asks how a concept needed in chapter two gets introduced in chapter one. Once the
universe exists, that is arithmetic rather than editorial instinct:

```
For every concept C and every unit U that uses C:
    introduced(C) = the first unit, in reading order, that defines C
    FAIL if position(U) < position(introduced(C))
```

A concept used before it is introduced is a pacing defect the build can name, per level, with the
exact pair of units. It does not tell you the writing is good. It tells you the reader was asked
to spend something they had not been given, which is the specific failure the memo describes.

The same structure serves instruction 8: reader feedback of the form *I did not understand this,
it became clear later* is a report that `introduced(C)` is later than it should be, and it
attaches to the concept and the pair of units rather than to a page number.

### A stopping rule for the universe

Building the universe first has one obvious failure mode: it never ends. A stopping rule is
needed before phase 1 starts, and it should be computable rather than a feeling. Proposed:

> **The universe is sufficient when every candidate plot line can be traced end to end through
> it without a gap**, and every spine candidate has converging evidence or is explicitly labelled
> an opinion.

That makes "enough" a property of the plot lines rather than of the material, which is the right
way round: the universe is not trying to be complete, it is trying to be **sufficient for the
story we are choosing to tell.**
