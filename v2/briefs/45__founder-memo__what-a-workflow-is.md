## The agent's reading

### What the memo asks for, in the order it asks

1. **The experiment is the phasing, not the subject.** The founder is testing whether a
   memo can be walked through the estate's own layers one step at a time: *"can you do this
   in multiple phases? So the first phase, like you do, you grab the transcription and you
   extract stuff. Then can you actually write one of the documents, like a source document
   based on this."* Phase one is this brief. Phase two is a source document written from
   it. Phase three transforms that document the way the pilot was transformed. Phase four
   connects the result to the book.

2. **Why he wants it: his own comment, quoted back.** *"one of the comments you made was
   that there wasn't a lot of workflows, documents, and reference material, right? So let's
   add it."* The gap is real and it is now measurable. The book's graph counts **27,002
   words** of prose across seventeen chapters, and in them *workflow* and *workflows*
   together appear **6 times**. The book is titled *Creating a Book Using Agentic Workflows*
   and has almost no text about workflows in it.

3. **The folder shape is specified, not left open.** *"It has to have its own folder, and
   inside its own folder, we're going to create the materials for it… you're going to create
   the JSON transformation of the file… you're going to extract the key concepts, and
   basically you're going to create the graph elements and the tissaurus and the semantic
   graph and the ontologies."* That is the pilot's folder, item for item: a source, a JSON
   decomposition, an anchored extraction, and the dictionary, thesaurus and ontology views
   projected from it. The instruction is *do to this what you did to Thinking in Graphs*.

4. **He asks for a name and the transcription hides it.** *"We can't need a name for this
   process"* is *we also need a name for this process*. The process is the one in point 3:
   a document arrives, gets a folder, and comes out as a graph. Two halves of it already
   have names in this estate and the whole does not.

### The definition, which is the reason the document is worth writing

5. **A workflow is how you operate.** *"what I call a workflow is fundamentally is how you
   operate. You know, is the the steps that you have."* Deliberately mundane, and the
   examples make the point: opening Word, writing, saving, sending is a workflow. So is
   markdown plus a save that triggers a PDF. So is this estate. The word is not reserved
   for automation.

6. **The 2026 claim, which is the document's central claim.** *"the power in 2026 with this
   agentic world. Is actually the agentic workflows that we can have, which is the workflows
   that allows us to change the tooling and to change the visualisation and to change the
   comprehension and to change the context."* Four things a workflow can now change that it
   could not change cheaply before, and one purpose for changing them: *"to allow the author
   to stay in the zone."*

7. **The measure of success, stated flatly, and it is a good one.** *"the ultimate measure
   of success is: Can I do a process without making any changes to the workflow? If I can do
   that, that's a major success… that's how I know something's been productized or
   commoditized."* A workflow is mature when you stop wanting to change it. This inverts
   the usual read of the six days: the releases that changed the machinery are not the
   achievement, they are the cost, and the achievement is the day the machinery is left
   alone.

8. **Explorer and villager are about impact on the workflow, not about skill.** *"depending
   on your programming skills and… experience in engineering, you can have more or less
   impact on the workflows that you have… The villager phase consumes stuff. The explorer
   also will create workflows. Will customise workflows. Will fine-tune the workflows."*
   The pair is a **phase** a person is in, not a class of person: he says *phase* both
   times. The same reader can be a villager on Monday and an explorer on Friday.

9. **The instinct is not to change the workflow.** *"my instinct is always not to change the
   workflow. My instinct is always, I want to be productive… But what I do is I allow myself
   to go there."* The trigger is noticing a manual step: *"I'm doing something that is
   actually quite manual… so I allow myself to go into that explorer mode."* And the bound
   is cost, both ways: *"there's always a point of diminishing returns here."*

10. **Sharpen the saw, with the payback period named.** *"you want to cross a bridge… and
    you realise, oh, I need a better tool to build this… Let me sharpen the saw… because I
    believe that in two years, two hours, or two days from now, I'm going to have a better
    saw."* The transcription's *"two years, two hours, or two days"* is a spoken correction
    landing on hours and days, and it is the honest range for a tooling detour in this
    estate.

11. **Maturing beats finishing.** *"we didn't finish all the workflows that we were doing
    before, but we matured them to a point where now in here we can now reuse some of them."*
    The WCLM is parked, not abandoned, and this is the memo that says why that is a normal
    state rather than a failure.

12. **The reusable-module analogy is the founder's own.** *"If you think about like
    development, you create classes, you create structures, you create web services, you
    create these reusable modules. That's kind of what I do, and that's kind of how I
    think."* Worth keeping because it is the clearest statement of why the estate is built
    as folders that can be lifted out.

### The book asks

13. **The Lego structure and the three-dimensional flow.** *"sometimes I operate on meta
    structures… That when we then put the the book together in a kind of Lego-like structure,
    eventually even drag and drop would be quite awesome… this book has a narrative, has a
    set of punchlines, has a set of pacing, has a set of introduction… almost like let's try
    to stick it up as a three-dimensional flow that our job here is to visualise it."* This
    is a **product ask**, not a document ask, and it is the first time pacing and punchlines
    are named as things to see rather than things to feel. The book graph shipped at v0.6.10
    is the data this would need; nothing renders it that way yet.

14. **Art of the possible first, restated with a reason.** *"the first book should start by
    the Art of the Possible, which basically means that a less experienced or less sort of
    used to these kind of workflows, user might consume that slower, and the more experience
    might consume that faster because he already has all the paradigms."* Brief 42 asked for
    the same opening; this memo adds *why*, and the reason is about reading speed, not about
    what a reader is capable of. **The same content serves both audiences at different
    speeds.** That is a stronger argument for one book than the one the record held.

15. **The villager can be an expert.** *"this book could be used by somebody with the same or
    more technical engineering skills than I do, but all they want to do is to just use the
    villager part of it… sometimes the difference between somebody with more or less
    experience is just that they consume materials faster, but they still go to the same
    steps."* This settles a disagreement on the record. The researcher's map found the book
    leans villager while brief 40 judged it explorer, and treated that as a contradiction to
    resolve. It is not one: the book is villager-shaped by design and an explorer reads it
    faster.

### The diff, which is a requirement and is not built

16. **The ask.** *"if you have started to make changes to the book, it's very important that
    we now go back to that diff view that we had before, and every time there's those
    changes, I should be able to read the changes between the two versions of the book and
    between two particular versions of a book."* He has read the published first version;
    what he needs now is the delta.

17. **The bar he sets for it, and it is the harder one.** *"ideally we need to be backed by
    graphs too. But even if initially just text based, but eventually should be a diff
    created from the graphs… because you should be delting the diffs of the graphs, not of
    the markdown. That would be the really test measurement of our success here."* Named
    reasons: markdown comparison is *"much more fragile"*, and a graph diff can tell a
    formatting change from a content change.

18. **What exists and what does not, checked.** A version diff exists for the **first
    edition** at `/v1/book/changes/`, built by `gen_changes.py` from the release tags, and
    it diffs extracted **text**. Nothing diffs the three v2 books, and nothing diffs graphs.
    The book graph that would make a graph diff possible landed one release ago at v0.6.10:
    seventeen chapters decomposed to 27,002 word nodes with a per-chapter identity ledger.
    The ledger is the piece that matters, because it carries an identity across an edit, and
    a diff of identified nodes is a different object from a diff of lines.

19. **The honest state of the ask.** The founder wants v0.1.0 against v0.2.0 of this book.
    Between those two versions **four files changed** — the front matter, the colophon,
    chapter 4 and appendix C — 30 lines added and 14 removed. A text diff of that is
    available from the tags today. The graph diff he actually wants needs a graph stored per
    book version, and only the current version has one. That is the gap, and it names a
    piece of work rather than an omission.

### One thing the memo does not settle

20. **Which book part one belongs to.** Point 14 says *"the first book"*. In this estate
    *the first book* has meant the first edition at `/v1/`, and brief 42 asked for the same
    opening in *this* book, the making-of. The reading taken here is that he means the first
    book a new reader picks up, which is the making-of, because that is the book brief 42
    was about. If that is wrong, part one is being planned for the wrong volume.
