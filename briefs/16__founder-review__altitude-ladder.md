# Founder review: the altitude ladder experiment

**Date:** 22 August 2026 · **Source:** voice memo, transcribed by Otter.ai, reproduced
verbatim below (per the house rule: the founder's voice is source material and is not

normalised into six items with the agent's comments and proposals at
[/reviews/r004.html](../reviews/r004.html). Unlike r001 to r003, this review's central
item was **executed rather than proposed**, because the memo asks for an experiment and
an experiment that is only described has not been run. It adds a new artefact beside the
book at [/altitudes/](../altitudes/index.html); no book content was changed.

**What it says, in one paragraph:** build five or seven versions of the book at
different altitudes, where the current book is the bottom level. The top is a paragraph
or a few paragraphs, an executive summary, and each of its phrases hyperlinks down into
the next level, which does the same again, all the way down. It has to be built
**bottom-up**, because each layer is a compression of the layer below that captures its
core ideas. Do it as an experiment rather than a commitment, and not necessarily for the
whole book: maybe get the top three levels right and take a couple of sections all the
way down, to see how it plays out. The number of pages should fall at every step. Two
concepts are in play and must stay apart: **the graph** (the facts, the evidence, the
structure, the ideas being conveyed) and **the projection** (the text itself and its
links). Each layer needs its graph; not every layer needs its projection. The structure
is tree-like as concepts merge going up, though the relationships can be many-to-many.
This also opens up translation, cultural adaptation, and versions aimed at different
stakeholders: technical, business, graph-literate. And the interesting part: once you
can see all the facts, observations and hypotheses a paragraph or a section is carrying,
you will find repeated content and contradictions between them, which then need a way of
being visualised.
